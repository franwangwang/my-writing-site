It means a complete beginner surfer. Wa and I started the book together a few days ago in Maui, and it's about this man Pedro trying to go from a kook to a big wave rider in six months (spoiler alert, he rode one gigantic wave, in the very last week). Pedro wrote that getting into surfing feels like a new beginning, and pondered how many times we really feel that in a lifetime. Having thought about it more, and focusing on only when I felt it in the moment (and not in hindsight), I think it's:

* Moving to the states and starting Wellesley (the exact moment is pretty vague now, but I imagine the feeling must have been there)

* Starting my first job at Greenhill (and met Diana that day!)

* Seeing my parents at my SF city hall marriage ceremony (yes it didn't feel real until I saw them, and they had surprise visited me)

* Quitting private equity, moving to Seattle by myself for Amazon after getting married

* Moving to Boston by myself for HBS

* Packing up everything I left at Francis' a few months after I moved out

* Seeing the finalized divorce papers (weirdly coincidentally on the same day that I went to city hall for the second time in my life for a friend's marriage ceremony)

* Going to burning man for the first time with wa (it signified something way more than burning man itself)

* Learning to surf in Hainan from the two surf girls I met in a horseback riding camp in Xinjiang

I had torn my MCL at the end of the surf week, which cut my Asia trip short, and made me miss my friend's wedding in Busan. And I think I did something to my knee again just last weekend in Maui after catching the first solid wave... But, I agree with Pedro, there's something about being out there on the ocean, so exposed and vulnerable, and learning to surrender to Mother Nature, and sometimes as short as a second of being one with the wave that makes surfing so addicting.

I have to admit that there's also this feeling of wanting to be cool that drives me to surfing. There's this physical part of looking cool (so cool), but I think the more appealing is the mental state of a surfer. The week I spent in 陵水 learning to surf, I was immersed in the surf community there: wake up at 6am to catch a sunrise session if the surf is good, teach a class at 8am, shower/lunch/nap, gather up with friends in the surf shop to shoot the shit or play Mahjong in the afternoon until it's time to go to bed. When the waves are good, then that's the priority. I think it's because when surfing, even if you are a pro, there's just so much that's not in your control - the wave, the wind, other surfers (especially kooks), the rocks, the reefs, that you have to chill out, otherwise you will get frustrated out of your mind before ever feeling any fun. And I aspire to chill the fuck out. In the ocean and in life.

In Maui, wa and I rented two 8' foam boards on day 1 of arriving - I probably could have used a longer one for stability but 8' fit in our rental car nicely. Wa was catching waves left and right on that very first day at the Cove, while I stood for a total of 3 seconds but still had a lot of fun being back on a board. I wore a rashguard but bare legged, and got so sunburned that the pharmacist at the drugstore stopped on his way out to help me choose a post burn spray. The next day I took a lesson, and despite twisting my knee again on the very first wave, still caught many more for the remainder of the class. I was nervous about my knee the entire time but also so proud. Day 3 we went to Ukumehame where Surfline had shown good conditions for a few days. But man it was rough. I caught a few waves on my tummy but spent most of the time just paddling back to the surf spot because the current kept drifting me down. I had to go back to shore and drink half a can of beer before building up the will power to go out again. Wa was annoyed too and I saw him raising his arms in frustration at a wave. Day 4 we woke up hungover (wa decided to feed us two whole bottles of wine the night before), so we thought we'd go for a sunset session. The waves at the Cove at 5pm were so weird - small and breaking all over the place and kept drifting us down. I waved the white flag 30 minutes in. So all in all, I put in a good effort and it didn't feel like I was winning, and strangely I kept wanting more. Pedro described surfing as a unique sport that gives nothing back in the form of validation, yet the pleasure is so real and mysterious. That felt so validating of my frustrations and thoughts of "am I insane to suck so much but keep wanting to learn?" I told wa that I want to bring him back to 陵水 to meet my friends and surf there soon.

Yutong had been telling me about this coffee shop called brewisland in Shanghai. I finally looked it up since I have half a workday to kill today, and saw that the decor is surf themed, hence the name. That seemed like my cue to pay a visit, even though it was out of my way to the airport. I showed up with my suitcase and saw it was a tiny shop with three counter seats and at most four bench spots by the window. A family was sitting at the counter chatting with the owner about this and that, so I patiently waited for my turn. Eventually I got to sit at the counter to chat with the owner, Chris. He's from Wuhan and moved to Shanghai in 2009 to work in branding for a few international firms, and left corporate a few years after for the coffee world. He got into surfing a few years ago and had also just got back from a surf trip. He said he loves being able to see the world via surfing. We instantly hit it off. I told him I've also been game planning leaving corporate at some point, and he shared how he got to open brewisland and found the sweet spot to serve the very top and niche coffee seekers (their coffees start at $10+). When I left for the airport, Chris gifted me a pack of their logo stickers (mountain and wave) and helped load my luggage on my DiDi. I loved their logo so much that on the ride to the airport I daydreamed about tattooing it to my ankle, but I'll sit on that.

On the last page of the book, Pedro wrote about just paddling on his surfboard in a lake back home in Colorado and dreaming about a wall on his right. I dreamed about surfing last night too.
